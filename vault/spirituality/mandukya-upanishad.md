# Mandukya Upanishad — Architecture of Consciousness

The shortest of the major Upanishads (12 verses). Among the most profound.

## What It Maps

The Mandukya maps the four states of consciousness and shows that the fourth (Turiya) is not a state at all — it is the ground of the other three.

## The Four States

| State | Name | Experience |
|---|---|---|
| Waking | Vaishvanara | Gross world, outward-facing, senses active |
| Dream | Taijasa | Subtle world, inward-facing, mind creates its own objects |
| Deep Sleep | Prajna | No objects, no distinctions, just undifferentiated bliss |
| The Fourth | Turiya | Not a state — the witness-awareness in which the other three arise |

## The Core Teaching

Turiya is not something you enter. It is what you already are. The other three states — waking, dream, sleep — arise within it and dissolve back into it. What you call "I" in deep sleep (the sense of "I slept well") is Turiya recognizing itself through the lens of that state.

The Upanishad equates Turiya with Brahman (the Absolute), and then with Atman (the Self) — the insight is that these are not separate things.

## AUM — The Symbol

The Mandukya uses AUM as the sonic map:
- A → waking state
- U → dream state
- M → deep sleep
- The silence after AUM → Turiya

## Relevance to Practice

Shambhavi Mahamudra works with this map. The practice moves awareness from the waking surface inward — through the dream layer of the mind — toward the ground state. What Sadhguru calls "the space between" is pointing at Turiya: the gap, the silence, the ground.

The interactive antarmukh tool maps the four states as cards with color-coded identifiers (waking: rust, dream: blue, sleep: violet, Turiya: gold). It's a study aid for navigating the verses.
