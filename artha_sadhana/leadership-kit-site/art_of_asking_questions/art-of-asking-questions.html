Questions are everywhere:

  • 💬 Daily chats with friends & family
  • 👔 Professional life – meetings, coaching, managing
  • 🧠 Even your inner thoughts (thinking = asking + answering yourself)

But the real question is:

"How EFFECTIVE are my questions?"

We get lazy. We assume we're good at it.
Spoiler: we're usually not.

   WE JUDGE OURSELVES      |    OTHERS JUDGE US
   BY OUR INTENTIONS       |    BY OUR BEHAVIOUR
        😇                |         👀

Ask yourself honestly:

     ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
     │  Do I genuinely want to         │
     │  UNDERSTAND the other person?   │
     │                                 │
     │  OR                             │
     │                                 │
     │  Am I trying to CONTROL,        │
     │  MANIPULATE, or FORCE my view? │
     └─────────────────────────────────┘

Golden rule:
If your true intention is understanding, the techniques you learn will build trust.
If it's manipulation, people will smell it a mile away.

The Acid Test:
In your next conversations, afterwards ask:
"What level was I listening at?"
Chances are, tricky relationships = stuck in levels 1-3.

   CLOSED QUESTIONS          OPEN QUESTIONS
   ════════════════          ══════════════
   Answer: YES/NO           Start with:
   "Did you see...?"        WHAT, HOW, WHY,
   "Do you think...?"       WHERE, WHEN, WHO, WHICH
        │                         │
        ▼                         ▼
   CLOSE conversation         OPEN conversation
   You LOSE control           You GAIN insight

The Hidden Trap:
In casual chats, people often answer closed questions as if they're open — they ramble on.
But when there's any formality or tension (manager/staff, conflict, dating), people shut down and give just "Yes/No".
→ That's when bad questioning habits bite.

Lesson:
Conscious questioning feels clunky at first. That's good. It means you're rewiring old habits.

   LEGITIMATE QUESTION:         DISGUISED QUESTION:
   "I seek to understand"       "I'm telling you something
                                wrapped in a question mark"
   
   Example:
   "Why didn't you talk        → Translation:
    to them sooner?"             "You should have talked sooner."
   
   "Don't you think you        → "You're wrong to challenge them."
    should ask first?"

Dr. Phil says: Up to 80% of our questions are disguised statements. 😱

Test yourself:
Ask a friend to be your "spotter" in a meeting. They note:

  • Genuine question → ✅
  • Statement in disguise → ❌

1. The "10 Open Questions" Challenge

   Partner answers YES/NO if you slip.
   
   You: "What's your favourite trip?"
   Partner: "Oh, I loved Bali..."
   You: "How did it feel stepping off the plane?"
   Partner: "Humid and magical!"
   You: "Tell me more about that first day..."
   ... keep going for 10 ...
   
   If you ask "Was it hot?" → Partner just says "Yes."  
   ➜ You go BACK TO ZERO.
   
   Frustrating? Yes. Revealing? Absolutely.

2. The "Find the Object" Game

   Back to back. Partner writes down ANYTHING.
   You ask questions to figure it out.
   
   BAD:  "Is it a tree?"         (test question)
         "Is it food?"            (closed)
   GOOD: "How would I recognise it?"
         "What's it used for?"
         "Where might I find it?"
   
   GOAL: Fewest questions wins!
   This trains you to ask OPEN, INFORMATION-GATHERING questions.
   You ask a great question.
   
   ┌─────────────────────────────┐
   │ SILENCE...                  │
   │ (2-4 seconds)               │
   │                             │
   │ What's happening:           │
   │ 🧠 They're thinking         │
   │ 🤫 You're not interrupting  │
   │ 🕳️ They often go DEEPER     │
   │    to fill the space        │
   └─────────────────────────────┘

Why it's magic:

  • No need to ask another question yet
  • Shows you're comfortable, attentive
  • They reveal more than they planned

Don't overdo it — no one likes an awkward stare contest. Keep it warm.

🔹 1. SPECIFIC Questions (Facts)

   "When did you join?"
   "How many in your team?"

Great for data. BUT: too many in a row → feels like interrogation, people shut down.

🔸 2. LEADING Questions (Manipulation in disguise)

   "Online learning is amazing, isn't it?"
   
   ⚠️ Use carefully. In interviews, you'll just hire a mirror.

🔹 3. HYPOTHETICAL Questions (Fantasy land)

   "What would you do if a customer attacked you?"
   
   They answer: "What does the textbook say?"
   Not what they'd actually do.
   Gets hypothetical answers.

🔸 4. PROBE / CLARIFYING Questions (The engine room)

   "Tell me more..."
   "What do you mean by 'difficult'?"
   "In what way?"
   "What makes you say that?"
   
   ✨ SHORT IS POWERFUL. No leading, just digging deeper.

🔹 5. REFLECTIVE Questions (The Jedi move)

   They say: "I'm struggling with the pot."
   You: "The pot?"
   They: "Yeah, the sequence of potting colours in snooker..."
   
   Just bounce a key word back with a question mark in your voice.
   ⚡ Keeps them in their own frame of mind.
   ❗ Don't chain too many → feels like a cross-examination.
   "WHY did you do that?"

          ▼
   ┌──────────────────┐
   │ FEELS LIKE:      │
   │ • Accusation     │

   │ • Threat         │
   │ TRIGGERS:        │
   │ • Defensiveness  │
   │ • Justification  │

Instead of "Why did you decide that?", try:

  • What attracted you to this option?
  • How did you arrive at that decision?
  • What makes now the right time?
  • What other options did you consider?

These open the exploration instead of poking the defenses.
