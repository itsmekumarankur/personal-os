Variable Pay & Equity

Bonus & ESOP Structuring

Variable pay looks like a reward but is taxed like salary. ESOPs have two distinct tax events — most people only encounter the second when it's too late to plan for the first.

Part 1 — Bonus
1. How is a performance bonus typically structured?

Usually a percentage of your fixed pay (e.g., "up to 15% of Basic"), tied to a mix of:

  • Individual performance rating
  • Team/business unit performance
  • Overall company performance against targets

It's shown in your CTC as Variable Pay — a maximum potential amount, not a guaranteed one.

2. How is bonus taxed?

Simple — it's fully taxable as part of your salary income in the year you receive it, at your normal slab rate. There's no special exemption or concessional rate for bonus income; it just adds to your total taxable salary for that year, and TDS is adjusted for the month it's paid (which is why a bonus month often has a sharp TDS spike).

Part 2 — ESOP Basics
3. What is an ESOP (Employee Stock Option Plan)?

A right (not an obligation) to purchase company shares at a pre-fixed price (the exercise/strike price) after a defined waiting period — regardless of what the share is actually worth by then. If the company's value has grown, this can be significantly cheaper than the market price at the time you exercise.

Part 3 — ESOP Taxation
5. When is ESOP actually taxed — at grant, at vesting, or at exercise?

Neither grant nor vesting is a taxable event. ESOPs are taxed at two distinct points:

EventTax treatment
1. ExerciseTaxed as a perquisite (salary income) — on (Fair Market Value − Strike Price) × number of shares, in the year of exercise
2. SaleTaxed as capital gains — on (Sale Price − FMV at exercise), based on how long you held the shares after exercising
This means you can owe real tax at exercise even before you've sold anything and turned the shares into cash — a common cash-flow trap with ESOPs, especially in unlisted/private companies.

7. Is there any relief for startup ESOPs specifically?

Yes — for employees of eligible DPIIT-registered startups, the law allows the employer to defer TDS on the perquisite value at exercise — instead of paying tax at exercise, tax can be deferred to the earliest of: (a) 5 years from the end of the exercise year, (b) date of sale of the shares, or (c) date you leave the company. This directly addresses the "tax due before you've received any cash" problem.

This deferral only applies to eligible startups meeting specific DPIIT and turnover conditions — check whether your employer qualifies before assuming it applies.
8. What should I actually think about before exercising ESOPs?
  • Do you have the cash to pay both the strike price and the perquisite tax at exercise — especially for a private/unlisted company where you can't easily sell shares to raise that cash?
  • What's the company's liquidity outlook — is there a realistic path to sale (IPO, secondary sale, buyback) within a reasonable time?
  • Does your employer qualify for the startup TDS deferral?
  • What happens to unvested/vested-but-unexercised options if you resign — most plans have a limited exercise window (e.g., 90 days) after leaving.
