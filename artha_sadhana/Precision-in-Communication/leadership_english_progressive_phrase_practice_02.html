LEADERSHIP ENGLISH — PROGRESSIVE PHRASE PRACTICE

Ordinary → Professional → Leadership → Executive

1. DISAGREEING

Level 1 — Ordinary“I disagree.”
Level 2 — Professional“I see it differently.”
Level 3 — Leadership“I'd like to challenge that assumption, if I may.”
Level 4 — Executive“I'd like to challenge that assumption, if I may. Help me understand what evidence we're using to support it.”

2. NOT ENOUGH TIME

Level 1 — Ordinary“We don't have enough time.”
Level 2 — Professional“We're tight on time.”
Level 3 — Leadership“That's outside our current bandwidth. Let's prioritize together.”
Level 4 — Executive“That's outside our current bandwidth. Let's prioritize together and agree on what we should deprioritize to make room for this.”

3. BIGGER PICTURE

Level 1 — Ordinary“Let's look at the bigger picture.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's step back and look at the broader context.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's zoom out for a second.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's zoom out for a second and make sure we're optimizing for the broader business outcome rather than solving only the immediate issue.”

4. ROOT CAUSE

Level 1 — Ordinary“We need to find the real problem.”
Level 2 — Professional“We need to understand what's actually causing this.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's make sure we're solving the root cause, not the symptom.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's make sure we're solving the root cause, not the symptom. Before we commit to a solution, I'd like us to understand what's driving the issue.”

5. DELAYING A TOPIC

Level 1 — Ordinary“Let's talk about this later.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's discuss this at another time.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's park that and come back to it.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's park that and come back to it once we've resolved the immediate priority, so we don't lose focus.”

6. KEEP ME UPDATED

Level 1 — Ordinary“Keep me updated.”
Level 2 — Professional“Please keep me informed.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Keep me in the loop.”
Level 4 — Executive“Keep me in the loop on progress, and flag anything that could materially change the timeline or risk profile.”

7. TAKE RESPONSIBILITY

Level 1 — Ordinary“I'll take care of it.”
Level 2 — Professional“I'll take responsibility for this.”
Level 3 — Leadership“I'll own this and keep you posted on progress.”
Level 4 — Executive“I'll own this and keep you posted on progress. I'll also make sure we have clear owners and next steps across the remaining workstreams.”

8. START WITH EASY WINS

Level 1 — Ordinary“Let's do the easy things first.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's start with the easiest opportunities.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's focus on the low-hanging fruit first.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's focus on the low-hanging fruit first—items we can address quickly and that could move the needle without introducing significant risk.”

9. START SOMETHING

Level 1 — Ordinary“Let's start.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's get started.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's get the ball rolling.”
Level 4 — Executive“We have enough information to move forward. Let's get the ball rolling and establish clear owners for the first set of actions.”

10. KEEP IT SIMPLE

Level 1 — Ordinary“Don't make this too complicated.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's keep the solution focused.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's not boil the ocean.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's not boil the ocean. Let's focus on the smallest set of changes that solves the most important problem first.”

11. GO DEEPER

Level 1 — Ordinary“Let's investigate this properly.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's take a closer look.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's do a deep dive.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's do a deep dive into the underlying data before we decide whether this is an isolated issue or a broader systemic problem.”

12. SIMPLIFY THE DISCUSSION

Level 1 — Ordinary“What's the main point?”
Level 2 — Professional“What's the key takeaway?”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's boil it down to what matters most.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's boil it down to the decision we actually need to make and the information we need to make it confidently.”

13. ALIGNMENT

Level 1 — Ordinary“Let's make sure we agree.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's make sure we're aligned.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's make sure we're on the same page.”
Level 4 — Executive“Before we move forward, let's make sure we're on the same page about the desired outcome, priorities, ownership, and what success looks like.”

14. DEFINE SUCCESS

Level 1 — Ordinary“What does success mean?”
Level 2 — Professional“What outcome are we targeting?”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's define what success looks like.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's define what success looks like before we commit resources, so we have a clear measure of whether the investment is delivering the intended outcome.”

15. CLARIFY THE OUTCOME

Level 1 — Ordinary“What are we trying to achieve?”
Level 2 — Professional“What's the outcome we're aiming for?”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's align on the desired outcome before we dive into solutions.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's align on the desired outcome before we dive into solutions. Once we're clear on the destination, we can evaluate the options against it.”

16. UNDERSTAND THEIR THINKING

Level 1 — Ordinary“Why do you think that?”
Level 2 — Professional“Can you explain your reasoning?”
Level 3 — Leadership“Walk me through your reasoning on that.”
Level 4 — Executive“Walk me through your reasoning on that. I'd like to understand the assumptions and evidence that led you to that recommendation.”

17. UNDERSTAND THE CONSTRAINT

Level 1 — Ordinary“Why do we need this?”
Level 2 — Professional“What's driving this requirement?”
Level 3 — Leadership“Help me understand the constraint you're optimizing for.”
Level 4 — Executive“Help me understand the constraint you're optimizing for. Is the primary driver cost, speed, customer experience, risk, or something else?”

18. WAIT VS ACT

Level 1 — Ordinary“Should we wait or do it now?”
Level 2 — Professional“What are the trade-offs between waiting and acting now?”
Level 3 — Leadership“What's the cost of waiting versus the cost of acting now?”
Level 4 — Executive“What's the cost of waiting versus the cost of acting now? I'd like us to make the decision based on the risk-adjusted impact of both options.”

19. PROTECT QUALITY

Level 1 — Ordinary“Don't rush it.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's make sure we don't compromise quality.”
Level 3 — Leadership“I appreciate the urgency, let's also protect the quality.”
Level 4 — Executive“I appreciate the urgency, let's also protect the quality. We should move quickly, but not in a way that creates a larger operational or customer risk.”

20. DON'T OVER-PERFECT

Level 1 — Ordinary“It doesn't need to be perfect.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's focus on what is good enough for now.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's not let perfect be the enemy of good.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's not let perfect be the enemy of good. We can deliver the highest-value version now and address the remaining improvements through a controlled follow-up.”

21. DISAGREE RESPECTFULLY

Level 1 — Ordinary“I don't think that's right.”
Level 2 — Professional“I have a different view.”
Level 3 — Leadership“I want to push back on that, respectfully.”
Level 4 — Executive“I want to push back on that, respectfully. I understand the intent, but I'm concerned that the proposed approach optimizes for short-term speed at the expense of longer-term risk.”

22. SEPARATE PEOPLE FROM PROBLEMS

Level 1 — Ordinary“Don't blame them.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's focus on the issue rather than individuals.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Can we separate the person from the problem here?”
Level 4 — Executive“Can we separate the person from the problem here? Let's focus on the facts, identify the process failure, and make sure we address the underlying issue without turning this into a blame exercise.”

23. UNDERSTAND THE OTHER SIDE

Level 1 — Ordinary“Tell me your side.”
Level 2 — Professional“I'd like to understand your perspective.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Help me see this from your side of the table.”
Level 4 — Executive“Help me see this from your side of the table. What are the constraints you're facing, and what would a workable outcome look like for your team?”

24. FLAG A RISK

Level 1 — Ordinary“There might be a problem.”
Level 2 — Professional“I see a potential risk.”
Level 3 — Leadership“I want to flag a risk before it becomes a problem.”
Level 4 — Executive“I want to flag a risk before it becomes a problem. We still have time to mitigate it, but if we wait until it materializes, our options will become significantly more limited.”

25. TIMEBOX THE DISCUSSION

Level 1 — Ordinary“Let's not spend too much time on this.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's keep this discussion focused.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's timebox this discussion so we stay productive.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's timebox this discussion so we stay productive. Let's spend ten minutes on the evidence, five minutes on options, and then make a decision.”

26. CLOSE THE LOOP

Level 1 — Ordinary“Let's finish this before moving on.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's resolve this before we move forward.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's close the loop on this before we move on.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's close the loop on this before we move on. I want to make sure we have a clear decision, owner, timeline, and next action.”

27. SET EXPECTATIONS

Level 1 — Ordinary“Let me tell you what to expect.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let me clarify the expectations.”
Level 3 — Leadership“I'd like to set expectations clearly upfront.”
Level 4 — Executive“I'd like to set expectations clearly upfront. We can commit to the actions we're controlling, but I don't want to create false certainty around an outcome we can't fully control.”

28. MEASURABLE IMPACT

Level 1 — Ordinary“This should make a difference.”
Level 2 — Professional“This should create measurable improvement.”
Level 3 — Leadership“This should move the needle.”
Level 4 — Executive“This should move the needle, but let's agree upfront on the metric we'll use to determine whether the investment is actually creating meaningful impact.”

29. IMPROVE THE STANDARD

Level 1 — Ordinary“We need to do better.”
Level 2 — Professional“We need to improve our standard.”
Level 3 — Leadership“We need to raise the bar.”
Level 4 — Executive“We need to raise the bar. The goal isn't simply to fix this incident; it's to improve the engineering standard so the same class of problem becomes less likely to recur.”

30. REUSE WHAT ALREADY WORKS

Level 1 — Ordinary“Let's use what we already have.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's leverage the existing solution.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's not reinvent the wheel.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's not reinvent the wheel. We already have a proven capability that addresses most of this requirement, so I'd rather extend it than introduce unnecessary complexity.”

31. HANDLE SOMETHING DIFFICULT

Level 1 — Ordinary“We have to accept it.”
Level 2 — Professional“We need to deal with it.”
Level 3 — Leadership“We may need to bite the bullet.”
Level 4 — Executive“We may need to bite the bullet here. It's not the outcome we'd prefer, but it's the most responsible trade-off given the constraints we're operating under.”

32. DEAL WITH URGENT ISSUES

Level 1 — Ordinary“We're constantly dealing with urgent problems.”
Level 2 — Professional“We're spending a lot of time on urgent issues.”
Level 3 — Leadership“We're constantly putting out fires.”
Level 4 — Executive“We're spending too much time putting out fires. We need to address the systemic causes so the team can move from reactive execution to proactive engineering.”

33. EXPERIENCE

Level 1 — Ordinary“I've handled this before.”
Level 2 — Professional“I've dealt with similar situations.”
Level 3 — Leadership“This isn't my first rodeo.”
Level 4 — Executive“This isn't my first rodeo. I've seen similar situations before, and the pattern I'd watch for here is whether we're treating the immediate symptom rather than the systemic issue.”

34. PERSONAL STAKE

Level 1 — Ordinary“Everyone needs to care about this.”
Level 2 — Professional“Everyone needs accountability for the outcome.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Everyone needs skin in the game.”
Level 4 — Executive“Everyone needs skin in the game. Sponsorship can sit with me, but execution ownership needs to remain with the people closest to each workstream.”

35. OUTSIDE AUTHORITY

Level 1 — Ordinary“I can't decide that.”
Level 2 — Professional“That decision is outside my authority.”
Level 3 — Leadership“That's above my pay grade.”
Level 4 — Executive“That's above my pay grade in terms of formal decision rights. I'll get the ball rolling with the appropriate stakeholders and bring back a recommendation.”

36. FAILURE TO FOLLOW THROUGH

Level 1 — Ordinary“Someone didn't finish what they promised.”
Level 2 — Professional“The commitment wasn't followed through.”
Level 3 — Leadership“We dropped the ball.”
Level 4 — Executive“We dropped the ball on the follow-through. Let's understand where the ownership or process broke down and put a clear mechanism in place so it doesn't happen again.”

37. REQUIREMENTS CHANGING

Level 1 — Ordinary“The requirements keep changing.”
Level 2 — Professional“The scope has changed several times.”
Level 3 — Leadership“We're moving the goalposts.”
Level 4 — Executive“We're moving the goalposts during execution. I'm happy to accommodate the change, but let's explicitly acknowledge the impact on scope, timeline, and capacity before we commit.”

38. ALIGN AFTER DISAGREEMENT

Level 1 — Ordinary“We don't agree, but let's do it.”
Level 2 — Professional“We have different views, but let's move forward.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's disagree and commit.”
Level 4 — Executive“We may not fully agree on the approach, but we've heard the trade-offs and made the decision. Let's disagree and commit, execute against it, and revisit the decision if the evidence changes.”

39. FIND HIDDEN MEANING

Level 1 — Ordinary“There may be something else going on.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's look beyond the obvious explanation.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's read between the lines.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's read between the lines here. The immediate feedback tells us what happened, but I want to understand what it reveals about the underlying process or incentive.”

40. THINK DIFFERENTLY

Level 1 — Ordinary“Let's try another idea.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's consider an alternative approach.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's think outside the box.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's think outside the box. Instead of asking how we can make the current process faster, let's challenge whether we need the process in its current form at all.”

41. MAKE PROGRESS

Level 1 — Ordinary“We need to make progress.”
Level 2 — Professional“We need to create measurable progress.”
Level 3 — Leadership“We need to move the needle.”
Level 4 — Executive“We need to move the needle on the outcomes that matter, not simply increase activity. Let's identify the two or three metrics that will tell us whether we're actually making progress.”

42. PRIORITIZE

Level 1 — Ordinary“We can't do everything.”
Level 2 — Professional“We need to focus our resources.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's not boil the ocean. Let's prioritize what matters most.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's not boil the ocean. Let's prioritize what matters most, explicitly identify what we're not doing, and concentrate resources where they'll create the greatest impact.”

43. PAUSE BEFORE ANSWERING

Level 1 — Ordinary“Give me a moment.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let me think about that.”
Level 3 — Leadership“I'd like to take a moment to think that through before I respond.”
Level 4 — Executive“I'd like to take a moment to think that through before I respond. There are a couple of trade-offs here, and I'd rather give you a considered answer than react too quickly.”

44. ACKNOWLEDGE A GOOD POINT

Level 1 — Ordinary“That's a good point.”
Level 2 — Professional“That's a fair point.”
Level 3 — Leadership“That's a fair point. Here's how I'd build on it.”
Level 4 — Executive“That's a fair point. Here's how I'd build on it: I agree with the underlying concern, but I think we should address it through a more controlled approach.”

45. FIND COMMON GROUND

Level 1 — Ordinary“Let's find a solution we both like.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's find a solution that works for both sides.”
Level 3 — Leadership“I hear the concern, let's find a path that works for both sides.”
Level 4 — Executive“I hear the concern, let's find a path that works for both sides. We may have different priorities, but there should be a solution that protects the core business outcome without taking unnecessary risk.”

46. SIGNAL VS NOISE

Level 1 — Ordinary“Let's focus on what's important.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's focus on the most relevant information.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's separate the signal from the noise.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's separate the signal from the noise. We have a lot of information, but only a few facts should materially influence the decision we're making.”

47. TEST THE ASSUMPTION

Level 1 — Ordinary“Are we sure this is true?”
Level 2 — Professional“What evidence supports this?”
Level 3 — Leadership“I'd like to challenge that assumption, if I may.”
Level 4 — Executive“I'd like to challenge that assumption, if I may. What evidence would confirm or disprove it, and what would change our decision if that evidence came back differently?”

48. END THE MEETING

Level 1 — Ordinary“Okay, let's finish.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's wrap up.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's close the loop before we move on.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's close the loop before we move on. We have the decision, the owners, and the next steps. I'll summarize the commitments and we'll proceed from here.”

49. FOLLOW UP LATER

Level 1 — Ordinary“We'll talk about it later.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's revisit this later.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's circle back.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's circle back once we have the production data. I'd rather make that decision based on evidence than speculate at this stage.”

Level 1 — Ordinary“Let's fix the easiest thing first.”
Level 2 — Professional“Let's start with the simplest improvement.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's go after the low-hanging fruit.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's go after the low-hanging fruit first, particularly the changes that can create measurable improvement without requiring a major investment.”

Level 1 — Ordinary“We have no choice.”
Level 2 — Professional“This is the most practical option available.”
Level 3 — Leadership“We may need to bite the bullet.”
Level 4 — Executive“We may need to bite the bullet. Given the current constraints, this is the trade-off I'd recommend, provided we're explicit about the customer and operational impact.”

Level 2 — Professional“Let's reuse the existing capability.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's not reinvent the wheel.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's not reinvent the wheel. Before we invest in a new capability, let's establish whether an existing platform or pattern can satisfy the requirement with a smaller change.”

Level 2 — Professional“Who is responsible for this?”

Level 4 — Executive“Let's make sure we have clear ownership. Every action should have one accountable owner, a target date, and a clear definition of done.”

Level 1 — Ordinary“Which one should we do first?”
Level 2 — Professional“Which priority should take precedence?”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's prioritize together.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's prioritize together. If this becomes the new top priority, let's explicitly agree on what moves down so the team isn't left carrying competing commitments.”

Level 2 — Professional“We have incomplete information.”
Level 3 — Leadership“I'd like to take a moment to think that through before I respond.”
Level 4 — Executive“I'd like to take a moment to think that through before I respond. We don't have complete information yet, so I'd like to distinguish what we know, what we're assuming, and what we still need to validate.”

56. CONTROL THE SCOPE

Level 1 — Ordinary“This is getting too big.”
Level 2 — Professional“The scope is expanding.”
Level 3 — Leadership“Let's not boil the ocean.”
Level 4 — Executive“Let's not boil the ocean. The scope is expanding beyond the original objective, so let's bring it back to the outcome we're accountable for delivering.”

57. HANDLE A FAILURE

Level 4 — Executive“We dropped the ball on this one. Let's own the outcome, understand where the process failed, and put the right corrective action in place rather than simply assigning blame.”

59. MIX-AND-MATCH PRACTICE

For each situation, produce a four-level progression.

  1. A senior stakeholder wants to launch tomorrow.
  2. Your architect proposes a complete rewrite.
  3. A developer says another team caused the incident.
  4. Your team says they cannot take another project.
  5. Product adds a new requirement one day before launch.
  6. The CEO asks for a guarantee you cannot honestly provide.
  7. The meeting has become a technical rabbit hole.
  8. The team has five competing priorities.
  9. A production issue has multiple possible causes.
  10. A project has been delayed because nobody owns a critical dependency.

60. THE GOLD STANDARD

For every ordinary sentence you hear during your workday, mentally ask:

“How would I say this at Level 4?”

Then upgrade it:

Ordinary → Professional → Leadership → Executive

Do this repeatedly until the Level 4 version starts appearing automatically.
