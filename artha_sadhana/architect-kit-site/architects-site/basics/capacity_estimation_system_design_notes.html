1. What is Capacity Estimation?

Capacity estimation answers one fundamental question:

“How much infrastructure do I need to handle the expected load while meeting my SLO?”

              +----------+-----------+

                 CAPACITY ESTIMATION

It is essentially back-of-the-envelope calculation. Exact numbers are less important than reasonable assumptions, correct order of magnitude, and understanding the architectural consequences.

3. Acceptable Backlog per Instance

Suppose:

  • SLO = 100 ms
  • Processing time per request = 35 ms
Acceptable Backlog / Instance = SLO ÷ Processing Time = 100 ÷ 35 ≈ 3

14. Quick Example to Memorize

Given:

  • 2M requests/day
  • P90 SLO = 100 ms
  • Request processing time = 35 ms

Step 1 — Average RPS

2,000,000 ÷ 86,400 ≈ 24 RPS

Step 2 — Acceptable load / instance

100 ÷ 35 ≈ 3

Step 3 — Required instances

24 ÷ 3 = 8 servers

Step 4 — Peak traffic at 4×

24 × 4 = 96 RPS

Step 5 — Peak instances

96 ÷ 3 = 32 servers

Architectural conclusion: design for horizontal scaling from approximately 8 instances at normal load to approximately 32 instances at the assumed peak, with autoscaling driven by load per instance and protected by sensible scale-out/scale-in policies.

16. Interview Checklist

  • Clarify the functional workload.
  • Estimate users and requests.
  • Convert daily traffic into average RPS.
  • Estimate peak RPS and state the peak factor.
  • Clarify latency/SLO requirements.
  • Use benchmark data for per-instance capacity where possible.
  • Calculate required instances.
  • Consider redundancy and failure scenarios.
  • Estimate storage and bandwidth.
  • Check database and cache capacity separately.
  • Explain horizontal scaling and autoscaling.
  • State assumptions explicitly.
