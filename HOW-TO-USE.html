Overview

What is this, in plain English?

This is your personal AI operating system. Think of it as a second brain that runs on Claude Code. You talk to it, it remembers things, processes what you learn, and helps you think.

It has two core parts:

The Vault — a wiki that grows every time you learn something. Every concept you study, every course you finish, every insight from your journal ends up as a page in the vault, cross-linked to related ideas. Open it in Obsidian and you see a graph of your own knowledge.

The Commands — slash commands you type in Claude Code that do specific jobs: run your morning brief, process a course, practice a system design, journal your evening. Each command reads your vault, does its job, then writes back what it found.

The vault gets richer the more you use it. That's the whole point — compounding knowledge over time.

One thing to understand: this system lives on your machine. Nothing is in the cloud by default. The vault is just markdown files in the vault/ folder. You can read, edit, and open them in Obsidian anytime without touching Claude Code.

Getting Started

First time — what to actually do

  1. Open Claude Code in this folder
    cd /home/isha/Desktop/CODE/personal-os
    claude
  2. Run /setup

    It will ask you questions about yourself, your writing style, and what kind of agent you want. Answer honestly. This fills in soul.md — the file that makes the agent sound like something, not someone generic.

    /setup
  3. Open the vault in Obsidian

    Download Obsidian (free). "Open folder as vault." Navigate to personal-os/vault/. You'll see your wiki pages and the graph of how they connect.

  4. Run your first /study-brief

    This is the morning command. From now on, this is how you start learning days.

    /study-brief

That's it. After those 4 steps the system is live. Everything else you learn on the way.

Commands

Daily commands — the ones you'll use every day

/study-brief Daily · Morning

Your morning brief. Tells you: what you studied yesterday, what to focus on today, which concepts from 1-2 weeks ago you should review (spaced repetition, manual version), and what's open and unresolved.

Run it before you open any course or Slack. Takes 2 minutes to read, saves you from starting the day aimlessly.

Also suggests today's 10-minute 100-Day Shift micro-habit based on what you're currently working on.

/reflect Daily · Evening

Evening journaling companion. Works alongside your existing daily journal in projects/leadership-kit/daily-journal/. You tell it what happened — sadhana, learning, work, what was hard — and it listens, asks 1-2 good follow-up questions, then saves what's worth keeping to the vault.

Not a form to fill. A conversation. Keeps it short — usually 5-10 minutes.

Saves insights to vault/me/journal/YYYY-MM-DD.md, updates course progress, flags connections to other vault pages.

Commands

Learning commands — when you're actively studying

/learn On-demand

The deep-process command. Takes a specific piece of content — a Udemy module, an article, a chapter — and really works it into the vault. Creates wiki pages for every key concept, links them together, updates the course tracker, and asks you one synthesis question at the end to check if it actually landed.

Use this when you finish a meaningful chunk of learning, not just as a quick dump.

Give it a file path, a URL, or paste text directly: /learn projects/architect-kit/AI/deploy_ai/day1-notes.html

/ingest On-demand

Bulk processing. Drop anything in the inbox/ folder and run /ingest. It reads every unprocessed file, creates or updates vault pages, archives the source to vault/sources/, and logs what happened. Supports PDF, DOCX, HTML, MD, TXT, even audio (transcribes first).

Use for batch dumps: 5 articles you saved, a folder of notes, downloaded course PDFs.

Interactive mode: /ingest (discusses each file with you)
Batch mode: /ingest --batch (processes everything silently)
Specific file: /ingest projects/architect-kit/AI/

/course-tracker On-demand

Shows you where every active course stands, when you last touched it, and what's queued next. Also has a /course-tracker next mode that recommends what to study next based on your current goals.

Run when you're deciding what to study, or to update progress after finishing a section.

/course-tracker — view the board
/course-tracker update — report what you finished
/course-tracker next — what should I study next?

Commands

Practice & review commands

/design-lab On-demand

System design practice. Get a real problem (not a toy — real scale, real constraints), work through it out loud, get structured feedback: what you did well, what you missed, what to study next. Keeps a practice log in the vault.

Use 2-3 times a week if system design is a priority. Builds the muscle. Reading about distributed systems is different from designing under pressure.

/design-lab — random problem at current level
/design-lab kafka — problem in a specific area
/design-lab review — review a design you already wrote

/weekly-report Weekly · Friday

Friday synthesis. Reads everything from the past week: log entries, journal pages, course progress. Tells you what you actually studied (not what you planned), what concepts clicked, what connected across domains, and what to focus on next week. Saves to outputs/learning-reports/.

Friday afternoon or evening. Takes 10-15 minutes including the conversation.

/career-radar Monthly

Career audit. Updates your skill inventory, runs a gap analysis against your goals, gives 3 concrete recommendations: what to study next, what to build to demonstrate a skill, how to think about your positioning over the next 6-12 months. Optional: searches for market signals in what's in demand.

Once a month. Not more — it takes real reflection to be useful.

Commands

System commands — keeping things healthy

/status On-demand

Quick health check. Shows the last few things learned, active courses, anything unprocessed in inbox/, and upcoming scheduled tasks. Under 10 lines.

/status for the snapshot. /status full for a complete system audit.

/lint Monthly

Vault health check. Finds: orphan pages (nothing links to them), stubs (pages with almost no content), broken wiki links, pages missing from the index, stale pages untouched for 90+ days. Reports what it found and auto-fixes what it can.

Run after a big ingest session, or when the vault feels messy. Otherwise monthly is fine.

/new On-demand

Build a new automation. Describe what you want, and Claude scaffolds the command file, the vault status page, and updates the routing table. Examples: a spaced-repetition reviewer, a book processor, a podcast transcriber, a note linker.

/cron-setup One-time setup

Schedules commands to run automatically using systemd (Linux). Once set up, /study-brief can run every weekday morning at 7:30, /reflect every evening at 9pm, /weekly-report every Friday. Each one runs Claude Code in the background and saves output to the vault.

Set up once after /setup is done. Schedules are in scheduler/schedule.md.

/setup First-run only

First-run onboarding. Fills in soul.md (your agent's identity and voice), seeds the vault from your existing notes, checks what tools are connected. Run this once. After that, all other commands work.

Core Concept

The Vault — how your knowledge actually works

The vault is just a folder of markdown files. But the way they connect is what makes it useful.

The key files

vault/index.md — the master catalog. Claude reads this first before answering any question, to know what pages exist. It's like a table of contents for your entire brain.

vault/log.md — the activity log. Append-only. Every command appends a line here. This is how /study-brief knows what you did yesterday and how /weekly-report knows what happened this week.

Wiki links

Every page has [[wiki links]] pointing to other pages. When you learn about Kafka consumer groups, that page links to [[learning/system-design/distributed-systems]] and [[courses/advanced-architects-lab]]. In Obsidian, these become edges in the graph. Over time the graph shows you which concepts are deeply connected and which are isolated.

What automatically gets added

Whenever you interact with Claude Code in this folder, it updates the vault without you asking. It learns you encountered a new author — creates vault/people/{name}.md. You mention a new concept — creates vault/learning/{domain}/{concept}.md. You complete a course section — updates vault/courses/tracker.md. You don't have to manage this manually.

Sources are immutable

When /ingest processes a file, it moves it to vault/sources/ and never touches it again. The original is preserved exactly. This is intentional — you can always trace a vault page back to the raw source it came from.

Workflow

The Inbox — how to feed the system

The inbox/ folder is the input queue. Drop anything there and process it later.

What you can drop in inbox/

PDF articles, DOCX documents, markdown notes, HTML pages, plain text, even audio files (MP3, M4A — it transcribes them first). Anything you want to learn from or reference.

Two ways to process

/learn — for content you want to deeply absorb. Interactive, asks you questions, builds concept pages. Use for Udemy modules and things you want to actually remember.

/ingest --batch — for bulk dumps. Processes everything in inbox/ silently and efficiently. Use when you've accumulated a bunch of saved articles or notes.

You can also give it a URL

Paste a URL in chat and it fetches the content, saves it to inbox/, and processes it. No need to download and drop manually.

Shortcut: You don't have to use inbox/ at all. You can paste text directly in chat and say "learn this" or "add this to the vault". Claude will save it to inbox/ then process it.

Honest Assessment

What it can do, and what it cannot

It can do

  • Turn anything you drop in inbox/ into structured wiki pages
  • Run your morning brief from your actual study history
  • Practice system design with you and give real feedback
  • Deep-process a Udemy module into 10-15 linked concept pages
  • Be your evening journaling companion and save insights
  • Track progress across all your courses in one place
  • Synthesize a week of learning into a useful Friday report
  • Keep a skill inventory and flag growth gaps
  • Cross-link everything — Kafka connects to architect-kit connects to AI leadership
  • Run automatically on a schedule via systemd cron
  • Read PDFs, HTML, audio transcripts, markdown, DOCX
  • Search your vault to answer questions from your own notes
  • Notice patterns across journal entries (e.g., the "observation/rush" theme)
  • Build new automations when you need them

It cannot do

  • Remember things between sessions without writing to the vault (it resets each session — the vault is the memory)
  • Proactively ping you — you have to open Claude Code
  • Watch your calendar or email without MCP tools connected
  • Sync directly to Notion without the Notion MCP connected
  • Run without internet (Claude API requires a connection)
  • Replace your actual daily journal — it complements it
  • Know what happened in your architect-kit or leadership-kit unless you tell it or /ingest those folders
  • Track what you learned in Udemy automatically — you have to tell it via /learn or /course-tracker update
  • Make design decisions for shivmarg.in or antarmukh — those are separate projects
  • Access the internet in every command — some outputs are purely from your vault
  • Guarantee perfect recall across very long sessions (context limits exist — it re-reads the vault to compensate)
  • Predict your future goals — you have to update vault/me/goals.md when priorities shift

The most important "cannot": The system is only as good as what you put into it. If you study a Udemy module but don't run /learn, it doesn't know you studied it. The vault is your explicit memory — it doesn't read your mind. The habit of telling the system what you did is the habit that makes it useful.
