LEADERSHIP ENGLISH — PRACTICE LAB 02

From knowing the phrase → to owning the phrase → to creating your own language.
The mission:
You already know the expressions. Now the goal is different: make them come out naturally when you are under pressure.

THE GOLDEN RULE

Don't ask yourself:

“Which idiom should I use?”

Ask:

“What am I trying to accomplish in this conversation?”

Are you trying to:

  • slow the conversation down?
  • challenge an assumption?
  • create alignment?
  • protect quality?
  • push for action?
  • reduce conflict?
  • clarify ownership?
  • bring people back to the root cause?
  • simplify a complicated discussion?
  • manage expectations?

Once you know the intent, the phrase becomes much easier to produce.

EXERCISE 1 — THE PHRASE ELEVATOR

Take the boring sentence and progressively make it stronger.

Level 1 — Ordinary

“I disagree.”

Level 2 — Professional

“I see it differently.”

Level 3 — Leadership

“I'd like to challenge that assumption, if I may.”

Level 4 — Executive

“I'd like to challenge that assumption, if I may. Help me understand what evidence we're using to support it.”

Now do the same for:

  1. “This is too risky.”
  2. “We don't have enough people.”
  3. “You are changing the requirements.”
  4. “Let's talk about this later.”
  5. “We need to fix this.”
  6. “I don't understand.”
  7. “This meeting is going nowhere.”
  8. “You need to do this.”

EXERCISE 2 — THE PHRASE FORGE 🔥

You're going to become a phrase creator.

Take a normal idea and combine:

INTENT + BUSINESS CONTEXT + POLITE FRAME + ACTION

Example:

Intent: disagree
Context: architecture decision
Polite frame: “I'd like to challenge that assumption…”
Action: ask for evidence

Result:

“I'd like to challenge that assumption, if I may. What evidence do we have that this architecture will scale?”

Create your own leadership phrases for:

  1. You think the team is solving the wrong problem.
  2. The deadline is unrealistic.
  3. A senior stakeholder is rushing the team.
  4. A team is blaming another team.
  5. The meeting is becoming too detailed.
  6. A project has too many priorities.
  7. A decision has no clear owner.
  8. A requirement keeps changing.

EXERCISE 5 — THE MEETING INTERRUPT

EXERCISE 8 — BUILD A PHRASE FAMILY

One phrase can generate an entire family of useful expressions.

ANCHOR PHRASE:

“Let's zoom out for a second.”

Now create five related phrases:

  1. A phrase to zoom in.
  2. A phrase to change the level of discussion.
  3. A phrase to return to the business outcome.
  4. A phrase to move from strategy to execution.
  5. A phrase to stop unnecessary detail.

Example thinking:

Zoom out → understand context
Zoom in → inspect detail
Outcome → clarify destination
Execution → decide next step
Stop detail → protect focus

EXERCISE 9 — THE “THREE QUESTIONS BEFORE THE ANSWER” METHOD

Before answering a difficult question, ask yourself:

1. What is really being asked?

2. What constraint is driving the question?

3. What outcome do we actually want?

Then answer.

Practice with:

“Why can't engineering deliver this tomorrow?”

Your internal process:

Question → Constraint → Outcome → Response

Build a 30-second answer.

EXERCISE 11 — THE “ONE PHRASE A DAY” CHALLENGE

Choose one phrase every morning.

Write it at the top of your notebook.

Use it at least three times during the day.

At night, write one sentence:

“Today I used ______ when ______.”

Example:

Today I used “Let's separate the signal from the noise” when the team started debating who was responsible for the incident.

EXERCISE 12 — THE RANDOM PHRASE MACHINE

Pick any number from 1–20.

Take the corresponding phrase from your original list.

Now create a workplace situation where that phrase would be the perfect response.

Then create a second situation where the phrase would sound awkward or wrong.

This is important.

Fluent speakers don't just know when a phrase works.

They know when NOT to use it.

EXERCISE 13 — THE 60-SECOND STORY ENGINE

Choose three random words:

  • customer

Tell a 60-second story involving all three.

Then tell the same story again using at least:

  • one idiom

This time, don't consciously think about vocabulary.

EXERCISE 14 — THE “NO BORING SENTENCE” DAY

For one entire working day, ban these sentences:

  • “Let's discuss.”
  • “I don't know.”
  • “I disagree.”
  • “We don't have time.”
  • “We'll see.”
  • “That's a problem.”
  • “Keep me updated.”
  • “Let's fix it.”
  • “That's impossible.”

You must replace each with something more precise.

At the end of the day, write down every replacement you used.

THE FINAL SECRET

Don't try to sound impressive.

Try to make the conversation better.

A good phrase:

  • reduces ambiguity,
  • creates alignment,
  • de-escalates conflict,
  • clarifies ownership,
  • forces prioritization,
  • protects quality,
  • moves the discussion toward a decision.

The vocabulary is only the tool.

The real skill is:

Thinking clearly → framing precisely → speaking calmly → moving people forward.

Eventually, you stop thinking:

“Which phrase should I use?”

And start thinking:

“What does this conversation need right now?”

That is when these phrases become part of your natural leadership voice.
