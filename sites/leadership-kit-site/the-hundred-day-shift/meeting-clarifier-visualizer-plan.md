# The Clarifier & Visualizer — Practice Plan
### Part of: Hundred Days of Leadership

**Skill target:** In any meeting — even when you're not the main speaker — take initiative to draw, diagram, or document the discussion in real time so everyone in the room understands what's being decided.

---

## Why This Skill Matters

A leader's real value in a meeting often isn't the loudest voice — it's the person who makes sure the room leaves aligned. This means:
- Actively translating verbal discussion into a visual (whiteboard, Excalidraw, Excel, doc)
- Checking understanding across all levels — junior dev to CTO
- Taking initiative without being asked

---

## Structure: 4 Weeks, Building in Difficulty

### Week 1 — Observe & Sketch (Foundation)
**Goal:** Build the reflex of visualizing, low stakes, no audience yet.

| Day | Practice |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Pick one meeting from your calendar. During or right after, sketch what was discussed on paper or Excalidraw — just for yourself. |
| 3–4 | Same, but try 2 formats: a flow diagram AND a simple table. Notice which fits the topic better. |
| 5–7 | Start a "translation journal" — take one technical topic from your Wealth/Demat domain (MF, IPO, NSDL, etc.) and explain it in 3 ways: for a developer, for a product owner, for a CTO. Write it down. |

### Week 2 — Practice Out Loud (Solo Rehearsal)
**Goal:** Build verbal fluency alongside the visual.

| Day | Practice |
|---|---|
| 8–10 | Record yourself (voice memo) explaining a concept with no visual aid. Then explain the same concept while sketching. Compare clarity. |
| 11–12 | Use Claude or ChatGPT as a role-play partner — ask it to play a confused junior dev while you explain a system design concept using a diagram you sketch. |
| 13–14 | Take one real upcoming meeting topic. Prepare a one-page visual (diagram, table, or flowchart) in advance — your "clarifier cheat sheet." |

### Week 3 — Live Practice (Low-Stakes Meetings)
**Goal:** Apply the skill in real meetings, starting small.

| Day | Practice |
|---|---|
| 15–17 | In your next 2–3 team syncs, volunteer to be the one who shares screen and diagrams live — even a simple box-and-arrow sketch counts. |
| 18–19 | After each meeting, ask one person: "Did that diagram help clarify things?" Note the feedback. |
| 20–21 | Reflect: What slowed you down? Speed of drawing? Not knowing the right format? Fear of being wrong live? Write it down. |

### Week 4 — Own the Role (High-Stakes Meetings)
**Goal:** Make this a visible, repeatable leadership habit.

| Day | Practice |
|---|---|
| 22–24 | In a cross-team or leadership meeting, proactively say: "Let me draw this out so we're all aligned" — even if you're not asked. |
| 25–27 | Build a lightweight template (Excalidraw board or Excel format) you can reuse for common meeting types (architecture discussion, incident review, planning). |
| 28–30 | Do a self-review: compare Day 1 sketches to Day 28 sketches. Ask a peer or your manager for one piece of feedback on your facilitation. |

---

## Daily Micro-Habit (Every Day, 10 Minutes)

Pick one real problem from work and explain it on paper in under 10 minutes, using at most one diagram type (flowchart, table, or box diagram). This is separate from the weekly plan — it's the daily rep that builds the reflex.

---

## Tools to Use

- **Excalidraw** — free, fast, great for live sketching in meetings
- **Miro** — better for structured, reusable templates
- **Claude / ChatGPT** — role-play different personas (junior dev, PM, CTO) to pressure-test your explanations
- **Excel** — good for tabular/data-heavy discussions (your natural strength given your domain)
- **NotebookLM** — good for turning your explanation into a Q&A rehearsal

---

## How to Track Progress

Simple 3-column log (can live in your Sankalp journal or this repo):

| Date | Meeting/Topic | Did I visualize proactively? (Y/N) | One-line reflection |
|---|---|---|---|

Review this weekly — look for patterns in when you *did* step up vs. when you held back.

---

## 30-Day Success Marker

By Day 30, you should be able to say: *"In at least 3 real meetings this month, I proactively drew or documented the discussion without being asked, and someone told me it helped."*
