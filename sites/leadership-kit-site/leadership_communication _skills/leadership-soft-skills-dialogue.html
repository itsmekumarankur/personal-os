If you want to influence, you don't just tell. You extract. You become a miner of insight, not a fountain of your own opinions.

The Secret:
Even in a pre-recorded video, you can create dialogue by involving the listener. Ask rhetorical questions, anticipate their thoughts, speak as if to one real person.

Your move:
Next time you present, don't present. Have a conversation with the room.

Ken's Car Story:
Driving to Glasgow, a friend kept interrupting. Ken reflected back: "It feels like you're somewhere else. Please take a moment and respond." After two minutes of silence, the friend admitted: "I was too busy thinking about what I wanted to say next."

If you're planning your reply, you're not listening. You're waiting to speak.

Demonstration snippet from Ken & Jason:

   Jason: "...we had to change our tactic..."
   Ken:   "Tactical change?"        ← Reflecting back
   Jason: "Yeah, strategy is the constant, tactics adjust..."
   Ken:   "So you're navigating change while keeping
           the big picture steady."  ← Paraphrasing
   Jason: "Exactly."
   Ken:   "You seem quite comfortable with that."
                                     ← Immediacy
   Jason: "I've learned change is a friend..."

This simple loop – reflect, paraphrase, notice emotion – keeps the conversation deep and the other person feeling truly heard.

The Bill Clinton Story:
A woman ranted at him about everything she didn't want. He stopped her: "I've heard what you don't want. Tell me clearly: what DO you want?" She paused, then: "Safer streets for our children. Better jobs. Drug dealers away." Now he had something to work with.

The Bike Analogy:

   Riding towards a narrow gate.
   There's a rock in the middle.

   Think: "DON'T hit the rock!"
   → You hit the rock.

   Think: "Get through to the other side."
   → You glide right through.

   What you focus on, you move towards.

As a leader, reframe:

  • "Don't be late" → "Be on time, ready to go."
  • "Stop complaining" → "What solutions do you see?"
  • "We can't lose this client" → "How do we deliver such value they'll stay forever?"

The "One Sentence" Check:
Before any important communication, write:
"What is the ONE thing I need them to understand?"

Not ten points. One.
Then everything else serves that one.

   These happen INSIDE people's heads.
   Yours AND theirs.
   They distort the message before it even lands.

Why a leader must know these:
When someone reacts badly, they're likely caught in one of these. Your job isn't to say "You're distorting!" It's to gently reframe using your 4 core skills.

  • "It sounds like one bad moment feels like everything. Tell me about what has been working?"
  • "You mentioned 'always' – has there ever been a time when it was different?"
   After a significant, challenging conversation,
   take 10 minutes and ask yourself:

   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ 1. What did I HEAR and SEE?                    │
   │ 2. What was I THINKING during it?              │
   │ 3. What FEELINGS came up in me?                │
   │ 4. What OPINIONS / BIASES was I holding?       │
   │ 5. What RESPONSE OPTIONS did I have?           │
   │    Which one did I choose? Why?                │
   │ 6. What was my INTENTION before I spoke?       │
   │ 7. What did I actually SAY and DO?             │
   │ 8. Would I like to be spoken to that way?      │
   │ 9. What EFFECT did my response have?           │
   │10. How would I do it DIFFERENTLY now?          │
   │11. What ASSUMPTIONS drove my response?         │
   │12. Was my agenda to empower or to control?     │
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

This is brutal honesty with yourself.
No one else needs to see it.
But if you do this regularly, your self-awareness – and your communication – will transform.

  • □ Practice DIALOGUE, not monologue.
  • □ Before any conversation, set your inner state: Available. Aware. Full attention.
  • □ Use the 4 skills: Paraphrase, Reflect, Listen, Immediacy.
  • □ Catch your own cognitive distortions.
  • □ Reframe negative "don't wants" into positive "do wants".
  • □ Write the ONE SENTENCE before you communicate.
  • □ After tough conversations, run the 12-question self-review.
   THE ULTIMATE TEST:
   "Would I want to be spoken to
    the way I just spoke to them?"

   If not, you have work to do.
   And that's okay. We're all learning.
