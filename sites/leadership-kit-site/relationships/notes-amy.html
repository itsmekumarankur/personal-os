5 The 70/30 Rule

Initially, don't talk too much about yourself. A useful rough target:

Conversation
┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                     │
│       HER            YOU            │
│      ~60-70%        ~30-40%         │
│                                     │
└─────────────────────────────────────┘

Not literally with a stopwatch. The idea is:

Ask → listen → respond → share something → ask naturally.

Not:

Ask → ask → ask → ask → interview.

Now it feels like conversation.

8 Good LIGHT Topics

These are easy conversation starters.

Everyday life

  • How was your day?
  • How has your week been?
  • Had a busy day?
  • What are you up to this weekend?
  • Did you get some time to relax?

Food

  • Have you tried that restaurant?
  • What do you usually like to eat?
  • Are you more into South Indian food or North Indian food?
  • I was thinking about trying X—have you tried it?

Movies / shows

  • Have you watched anything good recently?
  • What kind of movies do you usually enjoy?
  • Have you seen ___?

Travel

  • Have you been to ___?
  • Which place did you enjoy the most?
  • If you got a free weekend, where would you go?

Hobbies

  • What do you usually do when you get free time?
  • Are you into music?
  • Do you like cooking?
  • Do you read/watch anything regularly?

9 The GOLD: Remember Small Details

This is one of the strongest ways to create genuine connection.

Suppose she says:

"I'm planning to visit Bangalore next week."

Don't forget it. A week later:

You:
"Hey, you were going to Bangalore, right?
How did the trip go?"

That communicates:

She talks
   ↓
I listen
   ↓
I remember
   ↓
I care enough to remember
   ↓
She feels valued

This is far more powerful than constantly complimenting someone.

15 The "Don't Fix Everything" Rule

Suppose she says:

"Work has been really stressful."

Don't immediately say:

"You should do X, Y and Z."

First:

Her:
"Work has been stressful."

        ↓

You:
"Yeah? What's been happening?"

        ↓

Listen.

        ↓

You:
"That sounds exhausting."

        ↓

Then:

"Do you want my opinion or do you
just want to vent?"

That last question can be extremely useful.

16 Make Her Feel Heard

There is a major difference:

Hearing

She talks
   ↓
You wait
   ↓
You respond

Listening

She talks
   ↓
You understand
   ↓
You remember
   ↓
You respond to what she actually said

Aim for the second.

17 Don't Over-Compliment

Keep compliments appropriate and natural.

Good:

"That was a really good idea."

"You're very good at organizing things."

"That food was really good."

"You handled that situation nicely."

Avoid excessive personal/physical compliments. Keep the relationship warm, respectful, and easy — not something that feels like flattery.

18 Don't Force Vulnerability

Don't start asking:

"What are your biggest fears?"

"What problems do you have in your marriage?"

"Are you happy in your marriage?"

"What does your husband do that annoys you?"

"Tell me your deepest secret."

19 Very Important: Don't Create Emotional Dependency

Wanting her to feel:

"I can connect with him whenever I'm having a busy day."

That's fine if it means being a supportive, dependable presence.

But don't deliberately position yourself as:

"I'm the only person who really understands you."

That can create unhealthy emotional dependency, especially within a marriage.

Healthy:

"I'm here if you need anything."

          ↓

Support
          ↓

Respect
          ↓

Healthy relationship

Unhealthy:

"I'm the only one you can talk to."

          ↓

Dependency
          ↓
Boundary problems
