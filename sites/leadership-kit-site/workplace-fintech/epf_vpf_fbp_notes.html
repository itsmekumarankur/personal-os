Provident Fund & Benefits

Provident Fund — EPF, VPF, PPF & Flexible Benefits

Twelve percent of your basic leaves your salary every month before you see it. Understanding where it goes — and whether you should send more — is retirement planning at its most fundamental. Rates current as of FY 2025-26.

Part 1 — EPF Basics
1. What is EPF (Employees' Provident Fund)?

A mandatory retirement savings scheme, managed by the EPFO (Employees' Provident Fund Organisation), applicable to companies with 20+ employees. Both you and your employer contribute a fixed percentage of your basic salary + DA every month, and it earns compound interest until withdrawal.

It is not optional for eligible employees — it's deducted automatically from every salary cycle.
2. How much goes in — employee vs employer?
ContributorRateWhere it goes
Employee12% of (Basic + DA)100% → EPF account
Employer12% of (Basic + DA)3.67% → EPF account, 8.33% → EPS (pension)

So even though the employer also contributes 12%, only 3.67% of it lands in your EPF corpus — the rest (8.33%, capped around ₹1,250/month at the statutory wage ceiling) goes to the Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS), which pays a pension after retirement rather than earning EPF-style interest.

3. What interest does EPF earn?

The EPF interest rate for FY 2025-26 is 8.25% per annum (≈0.688%/month), declared annually by EPFO's Central Board of Trustees and approved by the Finance Ministry. It's calculated monthly on the running balance but credited once a year.

This rate is reviewed every year — it has stayed at 8.25% for three consecutive years as of FY 2025-26, but can change with future EPFO notifications.
Part 2 — VPF (Voluntary Provident Fund)
4. What is VPF, and how is it different from EPF?

VPF lets you voluntarily contribute more than the mandatory 12% of your Basic + DA into the same EPF account — up to 100% of Basic + DA if you choose.

EPFVPF
Mandatory?Yes (12%)No — your choice
Employer matches it?YesNo — employer contribution stays fixed at 12%
Interest rateSame EPFO rate (8.25% FY25-26)Same EPFO rate
Lock-inTied to employment/retirementSame as EPF

VPF is attractive because it gives EPF-level guaranteed, government-backed interest — usually higher than most bank FDs — on any extra amount you choose to set aside.

5. Is there a downside to contributing more via VPF?

Yes — one thing to watch: interest earned on your own (employee + voluntary) contributions above ₹2.5 lakh in a financial year becomes taxable in your hands. Interest on contributions within ₹2.5 lakh/year stays tax-free.

If your combined EPF + VPF employee contribution in a year is likely to exceed ₹2.5 lakh, it's worth checking the tax impact before increasing your VPF percentage further.
Part 3 — PPF (for comparison)
6. How is PPF different from EPF/VPF?

PPF is not part of your company's payroll process at all — it's a separate, self-managed account. It's mentioned here only because people commonly mix up EPF and PPF.

Part 4 — Tax Treatment
7. How is EPF/VPF taxed?
  • Contribution: Your own EPF/VPF contribution qualifies for deduction under Section 80C, within the overall ₹1.5 lakh 80C limit (only relevant if you're on the old tax regime).
  • Interest: Tax-free up to ₹2.5 lakh of employee contribution per year; taxable beyond that.
  • Withdrawal: Fully tax-free if withdrawn after 5 years of continuous service. Withdrawals before 5 years are taxable (with some exceptions like ill health, employer closure, etc.).
8. When can I withdraw my EPF, and what happens if I switch jobs?

You can withdraw fully at retirement (58 years), or in specific situations — unemployment for 2 months+, medical emergencies, home purchase, etc. (with partial withdrawal rules for each).

When you switch companies, the correct step is to transfer your EPF balance to the new employer's EPF account (linked via your UAN — Universal Account Number), not withdraw it. This keeps your 5-year continuous-service clock running for tax-free withdrawal eligibility, and keeps the corpus compounding.

Withdrawing (instead of transferring) when you switch jobs is one of the most common ways people accidentally lose both compounding and tax-free status.
Part 5 — Flexible Benefits Plan (FBP)
9. What is a Flexible Benefit Plan (FBP)?

A part of your CTC that you can allocate across a menu of tax-efficient components instead of receiving it all as flat taxable salary. You typically declare your FBP split once a year, and the amounts are reimbursed against bills or paid out under specific tax rules.

Common FBP components in Indian companies:

  • LTA (Leave Travel Allowance) — tax-exempt on actual domestic travel, subject to conditions and frequency limits
  • Meal coupons / food allowance — partially tax-exempt (e.g., Sodexo-style, ~₹50/meal historically)
  • Fuel & car maintenance reimbursement — tax-exempt up to prescribed limits if you own/lease a car
  • Telephone/internet reimbursement — tax-exempt against bills
  • NPS employer contribution (Sec 80CCD(2)) — employer's NPS contribution up to 10-14% of Basic is exempt, over and above 80C
  • Books & periodicals — tax-exempt against bills, in some company policies
10. Does FBP still make sense if I'm on the New Tax Regime?

Mostly no — most FBP exemptions (LTA, meal coupons, telephone reimbursement, books) are only available under the Old Tax Regime. The New Regime does away with most of these exemptions in exchange for lower slab rates.

One notable exception: employer's NPS contribution under Section 80CCD(2) remains exempt even under the New Regime — so this component of FBP still helps regardless of which regime you choose.

If you're on the New Regime, structuring your FBP around exemptions (other than employer NPS) generally won't reduce your tax — check with Finance/HR which components still apply to your payroll structure.
11. Quick summary — where does my money actually go? 
Your CTC
   │
   ├── Fixed Basic + DA
   │      ├── 12% → EPF (your contribution, 80C eligible)
   │      └── Employer matches 12%
   │             ├── 3.67% → EPF
   │             └── 8.33% → EPS (pension, capped)
   │
   ├── VPF (optional, your choice, on top of 12%)
   │      └── same EPFO interest rate, tax-free interest up to ₹2.5L/yr
   │
   └── Flexible Benefits (FBP)
          ├── LTA, meal coupons, fuel, telephone — old regime only
          └── Employer NPS (80CCD2) — works in both regimes
