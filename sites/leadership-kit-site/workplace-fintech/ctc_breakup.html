Salary & Compensation

CTC Breakup — What's Actually in Your Package

The number in your offer letter and the money in your bank account can differ by thirty thousand rupees a month. Every component has a reason — understanding them is the first step in making intelligent decisions about compensation.

1. What is CTC (Cost to Company)?

CTC is the total amount a company spends on you in a year — not just what lands in your bank account. It includes your salary, employer's contributions on your behalf, and the value of any benefits/perks, whether or not you actually "receive" all of it as cash.

2. What are the main buckets inside a typical CTC? 
CTC

 ├── Fixed Pay
 │      ├── Basic Salary
 │      ├── HRA (House Rent Allowance)
 │      ├── Special Allowance / Flexible Benefits
 │      └── Other fixed allowances
 │
 ├── Variable Pay
 │      └── Performance bonus / incentive (target-linked, not guaranteed)
 │
 ├── Retirals (employer's contribution — not paid to you monthly)
 │      ├── EPF employer contribution
 │      ├── Gratuity provision
 │      └── NPS employer contribution (if applicable)
 │
 └── Perquisites / Benefits
        ├── Group health & life insurance premium
        ├── Meal cards, LTA, other FBP components
        └── Any employer-provided asset (e.g., company car scheme value)
3. What's the difference between CTC, Gross Salary, and In-Hand (Net) Salary?
TermWhat it means
CTCEverything the company spends on you — fixed + variable + retirals + perquisites
Gross SalaryCTC minus employer's retiral contributions (EPF employer share, gratuity provision) — this is what appears as "salary" before deductions
Net / In-Hand SalaryGross Salary minus your own deductions — EPF employee contribution, TDS, professional tax
CTC
  − Employer's retirals (EPF employer share, gratuity provision)
  = Gross Salary
      − Your EPF contribution
      − TDS (income tax)
      − Professional tax
      = Net / In-Hand Salary
This is why in-hand salary always looks meaningfully lower than the CTC number quoted in your offer letter — a chunk of CTC never touches your bank account each month; it either goes to retirals or is a variable component paid only on performance.
4. Why do companies structure salary this way instead of paying it all as cash?
  • Retirals (EPF, Gratuity, NPS) are statutory or near-statutory obligations — not optional for the employer to skip once eligibility criteria are met.
  • Perquisites/FBP often carry tax advantages for the employee compared to receiving the same amount as flat taxable salary (under the old regime, at least).
  • Variable pay ties part of compensation to performance, aligning cost with outcomes for the company.
5. What should I actually check when comparing two CTC offers?
  • What fraction of CTC is fixed vs variable — a lower fixed component means more of your quoted CTC isn't guaranteed
  • Whether retirals (employer EPF, gratuity) are included inside the quoted CTC number or shown separately — including them inflates the headline CTC without changing your take-home
  • Whether insurance/perquisite value is counted at face value even though you may not "use" all of it
  • The joining bonus or ESOP grant, if any — these are sometimes shown as part of "total compensation" but aren't recurring CTC
Two offers with the same headline CTC can result in very different in-hand salaries depending on how much sits in retirals/perquisites vs fixed cash pay.
6. Where does a company-owned car scheme or FBP sit in this structure?

Both typically sit inside the Fixed Pay / Perquisites bucket, carved out from your special allowance — meaning they reduce your regular cash salary in exchange for a structured benefit, rather than being an "extra" on top of CTC. Worth checking your payslip to see exactly which component it's deducted against.
