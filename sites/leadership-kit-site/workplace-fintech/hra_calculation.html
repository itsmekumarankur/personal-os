HRA — The 3-Way Formula Most People Get Wrong

HRA isn't a fixed exemption — it's the minimum of three numbers, and two of those numbers depend on your actual rent and city. Getting the formula wrong means paying tax on income that doesn't need to be taxed.

1. What is HRA, and who can claim the exemption?

HRA is a salary component paid to help cover rent. The exemption on it is only available if:

  • You are a salaried employee actually paying rent for accommodation you live in
  • You've opted for the Old Tax Regime (HRA exemption does not exist under the New Regime)
  • You don't own the house you're living in (or the ownership situation doesn't disqualify you — e.g., you can still claim if you rent in a different city from a house you own)
2. What is the exact HRA exemption formula?

The exempt amount is the least of the following three:

#Component
AActual HRA received from employer
BRent paid minus 10% of (Basic + DA)
C50% of (Basic + DA) if living in a metro city, or 40% if non-metro
Metro cities for this rule: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai. All other cities (including Bangalore, Hyderabad, Pune) count as non-metro at 40%.

Whichever of A, B, or C is smallest is the amount you can exempt. The rest of your HRA (if any) is fully taxable.

3. Worked example

Suppose: Basic + DA = ₹50,000/month, HRA received = ₹25,000/month, Rent paid = ₹20,000/month, living in a metro city.

ComponentCalculationAmount
A. Actual HRA received—₹25,000
B. Rent − 10% of Basic20,000 − (10% × 50,000)₹15,000
C. 50% of Basic (metro)50% × 50,000₹25,000

Least of the three = ₹15,000/month is exempt. The remaining ₹10,000/month of HRA received is taxable as salary income.

4. What documents do I need to actually claim it?
  • Rent receipts (usually monthly, or at least quarterly)
  • Rental agreement
  • Landlord's PAN — mandatory if annual rent exceeds ₹1,00,000 (i.e., roughly rent above ₹8,333/month)
If your landlord doesn't have a PAN, they typically need to provide a signed declaration to that effect — check with your payroll team on their exact documentation requirement.
5. What if I don't submit rent proofs to my employer in time?

Your employer will deduct TDS as if the full HRA is taxable (no exemption applied) through the year. You can still claim the HRA exemption directly while filing your ITR — you're not required to have declared it to payroll — but you'll need to keep your rent receipts, agreement, and landlord PAN ready in case of a query.

6. Can I pay rent to a family member and still claim HRA?

Yes, in principle — for example, paying rent to a parent who owns the house — provided the transaction is genuine: actual rent is paid (ideally via bank transfer, not cash), a proper rental agreement exists, and the recipient declares that rental income in their own tax return. Tax authorities do scrutinize such arrangements, so documentation matters more here than in a typical landlord-tenant case.

You generally cannot claim HRA for rent paid to a spouse, since that's viewed as a shared household rather than a genuine landlord-tenant relationship.
7. Quick reference — when does HRA exemption not apply at all?
  • You're on the New Tax Regime
  • You own the house you live in (no rent being paid)
  • You receive HRA but don't actually pay rent
  • Your rent paid is less than 10% of Basic + DA (component B above becomes zero or negative, so nothing is exempt)
