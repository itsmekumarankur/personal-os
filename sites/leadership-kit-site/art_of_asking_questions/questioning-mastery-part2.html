🎮 Games to Play

1. The "Guess What I Wrote" Game

   Partner writes ANYTHING on paper.
   You: Find it using only good questions.
   Goal: Fewest questions = winner.
   Bonus: Forces you to avoid test questions.

2. Become a Question Anthropologist

   Watch TV/Radio interviews.
   Ask yourself:
   
   ┌──────────────────────────────────┐
   │ WHO is in control?              │
   │ Questioner or Answerer?         │
   │ WHAT types are they using?      │
   │ WHAT'S the impact?              │
   │ WHAT would I ask differently?   │
   └──────────────────────────────────┘

3. Conscious Awareness Mode

   Our brain is normally 1-2% conscious.
   Sometimes, deliberately flip the switch:
   
   🧠 "For the next 10 minutes, I will
       only notice QUESTION PATTERNS."
       
   Listen to others. Notice the TYPE.
   See the impact. Steal good ones.

4. Safe Experimentation Zone

   Friends, colleagues, family – 
   they're your lab. 🧪
   
   Try a new question type.
   If it comes out clunky → 
   immediately CLARIFY:
   
   "What I meant to ask was..."
   
   Poof! You just turned a bad question good.

Key Response Types – Cheat Sheet

Response What It Looks Like Why It Happens
Direct & Honest Clear, matches question They're comfortable. Less likely in formal/tense situations.
Lie Deliberately false Hard to catch. Look for plausibility gaps or nonverbal leaks.
Out of Context Completely irrelevant Avoiding the topic. Reword your question.
Partial Answer Picks only easy bits Formal/tension = more selectivity. Probe the missing parts.
Avoiding the Answer Statement unrelated to question Politicians' superpower. They say prepared lines.
Stalling "Hmm, let me think...", asks you a question back Needs time to craft acceptable answer.
Distortion Exaggerates, bends truth unconsciously Social norms, wanting to look good. They believe it.
Refusal Silence or "No comment" No legal way to force an answer. (Teens excel at this.)

🎯 The 5 Listening Targets

   ┌───────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │                                           │
   │   1. NOUNS ───────── (naming words)      │
   │   2. VERBS ───────── (action words)      │
   │   3. GENERALIZATIONS (always, never, all)│
   │   4. RULES/BELIEFS ─ (should, must,      │
   │                        have to)          │
   │   5. TENSE SHIFTS ── (past→hypothetical) │
   │                                           │
   └───────────────────────────────────────────┘
   They say: "I'll get the REPORT done."
   
   YOUR MIND sees:        THEIR MIND sees:
   ┌──────────┐          ┌──────────────┐
   │ 1-page   │          │ Verbal chat  │
   │ summary  │   VS     │ or 300-page  │
   │ by Friday│          │ tome by      │
   └──────────┘          │ next month   │
                         └──────────────┘
   
   SAME WORD. WILDLY DIFFERENT MEANINGS.
   
   🔍 LISTEN FOR: Vague nouns in critical areas.
   💬 ASK: "What exactly do you mean by 'report'?"
   "I'll HANDLE that problem."
   
   Handle could mean:
   • Ignore it and hope it disappears
   • Call a three-hour meeting
   • Fix it in 30 seconds
   
   🔍 LISTEN FOR: "I'll do it" – HOW specifically?
   💬 ASK: "How will you approach that?"
   TRIGGER WORDS:
   ┌──────────┬──────────┬──────────┐
   │ ALWAYS   │ NEVER    │ EVERYONE │
   │ NOBODY   │ ALL      │ TOTALLY  │
   └──────────┴──────────┴──────────┘
   
   "You NEVER listen to me!"
   "EVERYONE thinks this is stupid."
   "I ALWAYS do it this way."
   
   Reality: These are 100% claims.
   Life is rarely 100%.
   
   🔍 LISTEN FOR: Absolute words.
   💬 ASK: "Never? Can you tell me about a time
          when I did listen?"
   TRIGGER WORDS:
   ┌──────────┬──────────┬──────────┐
   │ SHOULD   │ MUST     │ HAVE TO  │
   │ OUGHT TO │ CAN'T    │ SHOULDN'T│
   └──────────┴──────────┴──────────┘
   
   "You SHOULD always ask permission first."
   "We HAVE TO follow the process."
   "You MUST be more careful."
   
   These are THEIR internal rules.
   Not universal truths.
   
   🔍 LISTEN FOR: Should/must/ought.
   💬 ASK: "What makes that so important to you?"
          "What would happen if you didn't?"
   
   ⚡ In conflict, these are GOLD.
   They show exactly where the divide is.
   Don't argue. Explore.
   You ask (PAST TENSE, real experience):
   "Tell me about a time you dealt
    with a difficult customer."
   
   They answer (HYPOTHETICAL, fantasy):
   "Well, what I WOULD do is..."
   
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ 🚩 RED FLAG! They switched frames.  │
   │                                     │
   │ You're now getting TEXTBOOK answer, │
   │ NOT real experience.                │
   └─────────────────────────────────────┘
   
   🔍 LISTEN FOR: Past question → hypothetical answer.
   💬 ASK: "I hear what you would do.
          But tell me about a specific time
          that actually happened."
   
   ⚠️ DANGER: If you're busy thinking about
   your next question, you won't notice
   the switch. Your brain will treat their
   hypothetical as real experience.
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │                                                    │

PRACTICE:

  • □ Play the "guess the object" game
  • □ Watch interviews, analyse question types
  • □ Go into "conscious awareness mode" randomly
  • □ Experiment on friends (they won't know)

RESPONSE AWARENESS:

  • □ Memorise the Response Zoo (8 types)
  • □ Notice when someone avoids, stalls, or distorts
  • □ Don't accept vague nouns/verbs at face value

DEEP LISTENING:

  • □ Hunt for: Nouns, Verbs, Generalizations, Rules, Tense Shifts
  • □ Trust the silence. Don't plan your next question.
  • □ Use the 10 Steps as your daily checklist
