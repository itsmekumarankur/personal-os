Income Tax Slabs — New Regime vs Old Regime

The new regime is the default and at most income levels it wins outright. But "most income levels" is doing significant work in that sentence — one home loan or a heavy HRA can shift the math entirely. FY 2025-26 / FY 2026-27.

Part 1 — The Basic Idea
1. What's the fundamental difference between the two regimes?
New Regime (default)Old Regime (optional)
Slab ratesLowerHigher
Deductions/exemptionsVery few~70 available (80C, HRA, LTA, home loan interest, etc.)
Who it suitsThose without large tax-saving investments/expensesThose with significant 80C/80D/HRA/home-loan deductions

Since FY 2023-24, the New Regime is the default — if you don't actively choose the Old Regime (via your employer declaration or ITR filing), you're taxed under the New Regime automatically.

Part 2 — New Regime Slabs
2. What are the New Regime slab rates?

Above ₹24,00,00030%

These rates apply uniformly regardless of age — senior citizens get no extra basic exemption under the New Regime (unlike the Old Regime).

3. Why is income up to ₹12.75 lakh "tax-free" under the New Regime?

Two things stack together:

  • Section 87A rebate: If your taxable income is up to ₹12,00,000, you get a rebate of up to ₹60,000 — which fully cancels out the tax computed from the slabs above, bringing your net tax to zero.
  • Standard deduction (salaried): ₹75,000 is deducted from gross salary before slabs apply — so a salaried person can have gross salary up to ₹12,75,000 and still land at ₹12,00,000 taxable income, keeping tax at nil.
This rebate only zeroes out tax up to exactly ₹12 lakh taxable income. Cross that threshold even slightly and you're taxed on the full slab structure from ₹4 lakh onward — not just on the amount above ₹12 lakh (this is where "marginal relief" provisions can soften the jump).
Part 3 — Old Regime Slabs
4. What are the Old Regime slab rates?
Income RangeRate
₹0 – ₹2,50,000Nil
₹2,50,000 – ₹5,00,0005%
₹5,00,000 – ₹10,00,00020%
Above ₹10,00,00030%

Unlike the New Regime, senior citizens get a higher basic exemption here: ₹3,00,000 for ages 60–80, and ₹5,00,000 for 80+ (super senior citizens).

Standard deduction for salaried employees under Old Regime: ₹50,000 (lower than the New Regime's ₹75,000).

5. What deductions are available only under the Old Regime?
  • Section 80C — up to ₹1.5 lakh (EPF/VPF, PPF, ELSS, life insurance, home loan principal, children's tuition, etc.)
  • Section 80D — health insurance premium (self/family/parents)
  • Section 24(b) — home loan interest, up to ₹2 lakh (self-occupied property)
  • HRA exemption — if you pay rent
  • LTA exemption — on actual domestic travel
  • Section 80CCD(1B) — additional ₹50,000 for NPS self-contribution
The New Regime removes almost all of these, except employer's NPS contribution under 80CCD(2), which remains available in both regimes.
6. Old Regime rebate under Section 87A — how does it compare?

Under the Old Regime, the 87A rebate applies only up to ₹5,00,000 taxable income (rebate capped at ₹12,500) — far lower than the New Regime's ₹12,00,000 threshold. This is one of the biggest reasons the New Regime is more favorable at lower-to-mid income levels unless you have substantial deductions.

Part 4 — Choosing Between the Two
7. How do I actually decide which regime is better for me?

Rule of thumb: calculate your total eligible deductions under the Old Regime (80C + 80D + HRA + home loan interest + others). As a rough benchmark:

  • If your total deductions are below roughly ₹4–4.5 lakh (varies by income level), the New Regime usually wins due to lower slabs.
  • If you have a home loan, pay significant rent (HRA), and max out 80C + 80D, the Old Regime can still come out ahead — especially at higher income levels.
There's no universal answer — it depends on your specific income and deduction mix. Use the Income Tax Department's official calculator or your Finance/payroll team's comparison tool before declaring your choice for the year.
8. Can I switch regimes every year?

If you only have salary income (no business/profession income), yes — you can choose your regime freely every financial year, either through your employer's declaration at the start of the year or while filing your ITR.

If you have business or professional income, switching is more restricted — once you opt out of the New Regime, you can only switch back to it once in your lifetime.

9. What about surcharge and cess — do these apply on top?

Yes, in both regimes:

  • Health & Education Cess: 4% on (tax + surcharge), for all taxpayers regardless of income.
  • Surcharge: Applies only above ₹50 lakh total income, on a rising scale. The New Regime caps maximum surcharge at 25%; the Old Regime allows it to go up to 37% for income above ₹5 crore.
