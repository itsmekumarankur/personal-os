Company Car Policy

Company-Owned Car (COC) Policy — How It Works

The car is in your driveway. The RC says the company's name. This distinction is consequential in at least three ways — only one of which is about traffic checks.

Part 1 — The Basics
1. What is a Company-Owned Car?

A car that is:

  • Purchased and financed in the company's name (not the employee's).
  • Given to an employee for official and personal use.
  • Paid off gradually through salary deductions (EMI-style), over a fixed tenure — commonly 3 to 5 years.

The employee uses the car. The company owns it, until an ownership transfer happens at (or before) the end of the scheme.

Part 2 — Registration, RC, and Documents
4. What happens to the car's RC (Registration Certificate)?

The RC is issued in the company's name at the time of purchase — the company is recorded as the "registered owner" with the RTO, exactly like any car buyer would be.

It stays that way for the entire tenure. It does not automatically move to the employee's name just because EMIs are being deducted from their salary.

This matters practically: if you're stopped for a traffic check, the RC will show the company as owner, not you. Your employee ID / authorization letter / COC allotment letter is usually what establishes you as the authorized user.
5. How does the RC eventually transfer to the employee?

Once the scheme completes (loan fully paid + any residual/buyout amount settled), the standard process is:

Loan closed + residual paid
        │
        ▼
Company issues a "No Objection Certificate" (NOC)
        │
        ▼
Form 29 & Form 30 filed with RTO
(transfer of ownership — seller = company, buyer = employee)
        │
        ▼
RC updated with employee's name
        │
        ▼
Employee is now the legal owner

This is the same RTO ownership-transfer process used for any used-car sale in India — the company is technically "selling" the car to the employee at the agreed residual/buyout price.

The employee usually also needs to arrange a fresh insurance policy in their own name at this point, since the old policy was in the company's name.
6. Does my driving license need anything different for a COC?

No — your driving license is unaffected. It's tied to you as an individual, not to who owns the vehicle. You need a valid license to drive the car exactly as you would for any car, company-owned or personal.

What does matter is that if there's a traffic violation or accident, liability generally falls on the driver (you), even though the RC shows the company as owner — this is usually spelled out in the COC agreement you sign.

7. Who is responsible for insurance, service, and fuel during the scheme?

Varies by company policy, but common patterns:

CostCommon arrangement
InsuranceCompany pays, or recovers via salary deduction
Servicing/maintenanceEmployee pays and claims reimbursement, or a fixed allowance
FuelFuel card / monthly limit / employee bears it — varies widely

At completion, the employee typically pays a final residual value / buyout amount (a lump sum decided upfront) to close out the scheme, after which ownership transfers via the RTO process in Q5.

Part 4 — Leaving the Company Early
9. What generally happens if I resign before the tenure ends?

Because the loan is tied to your employment (salary deduction + company guarantee), it can't simply continue after you leave. Companies typically offer one of these paths at exit:

  • Settle & keep — pay off the outstanding loan amount (plus any foreclosure charge), then go through the same NOC + RTO transfer process as a normal completion.
  • Return the car — hand the vehicle back; the company revalues or sells it, and any shortfall between its value and your outstanding loan is billed to you (surplus refund policies vary by company).
The exact rule is always defined in the COC agreement you sign at allotment — this is the single most important document to check.
10. How is the "amount owed" calculated if I leave early?

It's based on the outstanding loan principal at that point in time — not simply "remaining EMIs × EMI amount," because each EMI includes future interest that hasn't been incurred yet.

Since EMIs are front-loaded with interest (standard reducing-balance amortization), the outstanding principal after, say, half the tenure is usually more than half the original loan — even though half the EMIs have been paid.

11. Can I sell or modify the car during the scheme?

No, generally not — since the company is the legal owner (RC holder), the employee isn't authorized to sell, modify, or transfer the vehicle without company approval.

Part 5 — Before You Sign Up
12. What should I check in my COC agreement upfront?
  • Interest rate and full amortization schedule
  • Residual/buyout value and when it's payable
  • Exact exit clause — settlement formula if you resign early
  • Whether there's a foreclosure/processing charge
  • Who bears insurance, servicing, and fuel costs
  • What documents you'll receive (NOC, Form 29/30, loan closure letter) at ownership transfer
