Tax Filing & TDS

Form 16 & TDS on Salary

TDS is your employer's best estimate of your annual tax, collected in monthly installments. Form 16 is the certificate that proves the estimate was acted on. The gap between them is what your ITR exists to resolve.

Part 1 — TDS on Salary
1. What is TDS on salary?

TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) is the mechanism by which your employer deducts your estimated income tax every month directly from salary and deposits it with the government on your behalf — instead of you paying the full year's tax in one go.

It's an advance, ongoing collection of the tax you'll eventually owe for the year, not a separate tax in itself.

This is why your TDS amount can change mid-year — if you submit late investment proofs, get a bonus, or change your declared regime, the employer recalculates and adjusts TDS for the remaining months.

3. What if my actual tax liability differs from what was deducted?
  • Too much deducted (e.g., you had extra deductions you didn't declare to payroll) → claim a refund when filing your ITR.
  • Too little deducted (e.g., you have other income like freelance/interest not known to payroll) → pay the balance as self-assessment tax before filing your return.
TDS is an estimate reconciled at year-end through your ITR — it's normal for the final number to differ slightly from what was deducted monthly.
4. Does TDS depend on which tax regime I choose?

Yes — you declare your regime preference to your employer (usually at the start of the financial year), and TDS is calculated using that regime's slabs. You can change your declaration for TDS purposes with your employer, and separately, you can choose a different regime while actually filing your ITR (for salary-only taxpayers) — the final regime used in your return is what ultimately governs your tax liability, TDS is just the running estimate.

Part 2 — Form 16
5. What is Form 16?

Form 16 is the annual TDS certificate your employer is legally required to issue after the financial year ends (typically by 15th June), summarizing all the tax deducted from your salary during that year. It's your primary supporting document while filing your ITR.

6. What's the difference between Part A and Part B of Form 16?

Part A confirms tax was actually deposited with the government (traceable via your PAN); Part B shows how your taxable income and final tax figure were arrived at.

7. What should I check in my Form 16 before filing my ITR?
  • PAN details are correct — errors here can cause TDS mismatch
  • Gross salary and taxable income match your own calculation/payslips
  • All declared deductions (80C, 80D, HRA, home loan interest, etc.) are correctly reflected
  • Total TDS in Form 16 matches the TDS shown in Form 26AS / AIS (the government's own record) — a mismatch here can delay refunds or trigger notices
If you switched jobs mid-year, you'll get a separate Form 16 from each employer — you need to combine both while filing your return, since each employer only sees the income they paid you.
8. What if I switched jobs mid-year — does TDS still work correctly?

Not automatically. Each employer calculates TDS assuming they are your only source of income for the year, applying the full basic exemption and slab benefit independently. If you don't declare your previous employer's salary to your new employer (via Form 12B), your combined income can end up under-taxed through the year — leading to a larger tax payable when you file your return.

Best practice: share your previous employer's salary and TDS details with your new employer using Form 12B, so the new employer computes TDS on your combined annual income correctly.
