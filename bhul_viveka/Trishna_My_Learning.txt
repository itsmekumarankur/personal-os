                 TRISHNA-N - MY LEARNING
     (A note to myself, to read whenever the feeling returns)
==================================================================

Date written: September 2026

The project was never really about the project. It was Trishna wearing a disguise, a reason to talk to her more. And that's exactly why it hurts your dignity, because deep down you know the real reason, and so you keep chasing, poking, almost begging, while she stays uninterested. A man doesn't lose his dignity by having a feeling. He loses it by chasing something that keeps walking away. And he gets it all back the moment he stops chasing.

So the way forward is clear, let the project go. Not with drama, no big announcement. Just quietly stop pushing it. If it dies from her lack of interest, let it die. Your energy, your talent, sixteen years of building real systems, that belongs to your job hunt and your own growth, not to a project designed just to earn her attention.

SHE IS LEAST INTERESTED - BCOZ she might get full support from his office team and she do not need my support at all. She is also does not seem interested in projects for her daughter which I am helping her.

You're learning the single most important skill a leader can have, mastery over your own inner world. The situation with your sis isn't just a personal problem, it's your training ground. What you control is your reaction, not the feeling itself. Don't suppress the feeling, that only makes it stronger. Instead, notice it like a passing cloud, name it, and choose not to act on it. No daily messages, no waiting for replies, no surprise gestures. Keep everything transparent with Bulbul, because trust is your foundation. With the relationship itself, stay warm and normal at family gatherings, but drop the expectation of getting anything back, that expectation is what creates the anxiety. Don't build walls by avoiding trips, build freedom by showing up and staying grounded inside.

And here's the deeper lesson, the same muscle that lets you sit with a craving and not react is exactly the muscle that makes a calm, trusted leader under pressure. Your emotional control, your spiritual growth, and your leadership are all the same path. This hard month isn't a detour, it's the classroom.

Let's call it "Trishna". In Sanskrit it means thirst, the kind that never gets quenched no matter how much you drink. That's exactly what you described: one hour, two hours, and the mind still wants more. It has no end, because it was never meant to be filled from outside.

So whenever it shows up, the urge to message her, waiting for her reply, wanting to stretch a conversation, planning a gesture Bulbul doesn't know about, just say to yourself, "Ah, Trishna is here." Naming it creates a small gap between the feeling and you. In that gap, you get to choose. You don't fight it, you don't feed it, you just watch it pass.

And a simple reminder to go with it: "Trishna asks for more, it never asks for enough."

Every time you catch it and let it pass without acting, you're doing your sadhana, and building the same calm that'll make you a steadier leader.
